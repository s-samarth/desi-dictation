# Bhashini

> **Layer:** Indian model / public infrastructure · **Verdict:** the government's free language-AI utility — not a product rival, but it sets the *political and price floor* ("the state offers this free") and is a possible free model/API supply for us.

## Snapshot

| | |
|---|---|
| **Org** | BHASHINI — Government of India, National Language Translation Mission (launched 2022) |
| **Category** | India's shared **Digital Public Infrastructure** for language AI |
| **Capabilities** | ASR, machine translation, TTS, speech-to-speech translation, OCR, transliteration — via open, interoperable APIs |
| **Pricing** | **Free / public-good** (government-funded DPI) |
| **Mission** | Connect 1.4B citizens to services across Indian languages |
| **Confidence** | Medium-high — government + 2026 coverage |

## What it is

A state-built, free, open API layer for Indian-language AI, meant as public infrastructure (like UPI for language). It aggregates models (some its own, some from partners like AI4Bharat) behind common APIs and pushes them into government and citizen services. It is not a consumer app and not a company chasing our market — but it shapes the landscape in two ways that matter: it normalizes "Indian-language AI should be free," and it offers a *free supply* of ASR/MT/transliteration we could, in principle, consume.

## Where it intersects us

- **Free-floor effect:** when the government provides free ASR/translation, it anchors public expectation toward "why pay?" — a headwind for *any* paid Indian-language tool (relevant to MONETIZATION.md's pricing psychology).
- **Supply option:** Bhashini's transliteration and ASR APIs are free and could theoretically back features — but they're cloud APIs (against our on-device brand) and government-service-oriented (uptime/quality/consumer-polish not guaranteed).
- **Devanagari/formal orientation:** built for citizen-service use (forms, government content), not Roman-script casual Hinglish texting — far from our consumer use case.

## Strengths

Free; government backing and mandate; broad capability set; interoperable APIs; legitimizes and funds the whole Indian-language-AI ecosystem; huge potential reach through public services.

## Weaknesses (for our purposes)

- **Not a consumer product** — no dictation UX, no polish, no brand people choose for daily texting.
- **Cloud API** — antithetical to on-device privacy.
- **Formal/Devanagari/government orientation** — wrong register and script for "text like you text your friends."
- Government-service quality/latency variability; not built for a snappy dictation loop.

## Threat to us & how we differentiate

**Threat: low, indirect.** Bhashini won't build a consumer Hinglish dictation app. Its only real pressure on us is psychological/political — the "it should be free" expectation it reinforces. We differentiate the same way every paid product differentiates from a free government utility: consumer craft, privacy (on-device vs. their cloud), the right register (casual Roman Hinglish vs. formal Devanagari), reliability, and a focused daily-use experience.

**Opportunity:** cite Bhashini/AI4Bharat lineage as *credibility* ("built on the same open Indian-language-AI foundations the government backs") without depending on Bhashini's cloud. And watch it as a barometer: if Bhashini ever ships a slick free consumer dictation front-end, the free-floor pressure becomes real — until then it's context, not competition.
