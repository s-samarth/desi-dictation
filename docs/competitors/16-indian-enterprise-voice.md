# Indian Enterprise Voice (Reverie · Gnani.ai · BharatGen)

> **Layer:** Indian model / enterprise voice · **Verdict:** the B2B/enterprise Indian-voice field — not consumer-dictation rivals, but they contest the *enterprise* revenue we eyed in MONETIZATION.md and shape the Indian voice-AI talent/model landscape. Grouped because none is a direct consumer competitor individually.

## The players

### Reverie Language Technologies (Reliance-acquired)
- Long-standing Indian language-tech company (localization, transliteration, IME/keyboards, ASR/TTS), now inside the **Reliance** ecosystem.
- Strength: deep Indian-language localization + transliteration heritage; enterprise/telecom distribution via Reliance (Jio's reach is enormous).
- Relevance: transliteration (Roman↔Devanagari) is core to them — the exact operation our convention/normalization touches. A Jio-backed consumer voice push would have distribution rivaling Google's in India.

### Gnani.ai
- Voice-AI company focused on **speech recognition for regional accents**, conversational voice bots, and enterprise voice automation (call centers, banking).
- Part of the **BharatGen** consortium (IIT-Bombay-led) building sovereign Indian language + voice models governed/deployed within India.
- Relevance: accent-robust ASR + enterprise voice; a supplier/benchmark, and an enterprise-sales competitor if we chase call-center-adjacent B2B.

### BharatGen (IIT-Bombay-led consortium)
- Government-adjacent effort to build Indian LLMs and voice models trained/deployed/governed entirely in India (sovereign-AI theme, alongside Sarvam and Bhashini).
- Relevance: another open/sovereign model source and a signal of how much national energy is behind Indian voice AI (validation + future model supply).

## What they have in common

- **Enterprise/B2B orientation** — call centers, banking, telecom, government. They sell voice *automation and transcription* to businesses, not a consumer dictation app.
- **Cloud/on-prem enterprise deployment**, not privacy-on-device consumer.
- **Accent/regional-language depth** and real Indian-speech data.
- **Not chasing Rekha/Rohan/Aman** with a daily-driver dictation product.

## Strengths (collectively)

Enterprise distribution (Reliance/Jio for Reverie especially); real Indian-speech data and accent robustness; sovereign-AI tailwind and funding; established B2B sales motions; model-building capacity.

## Weaknesses (vs. our niche)

- No consumer dictation product; no Roman-script "text like you text" focus.
- Cloud/enterprise deployment — not on-device consumer privacy.
- Enterprise sales cycles and integrations, not viral consumer craft.

## Threat to us & how we differentiate

**Threat: low on consumer, real on enterprise.** These are the players we'd actually bump into if we pursue the B2B path from MONETIZATION.md (§2.10) — a law firm or clinic evaluating Indian voice transcription might see Gnani/Reverie-class vendors. But those vendors sell *cloud/enterprise platforms with integration projects*, whereas our enterprise pitch is the opposite: a **zero-integration, on-device, per-seat license** where "there is nothing to audit because nothing leaves the device." For a small firm that wants dictation-on-laptops without a data-processing agreement, we're simpler and cheaper; for a bank building a voice-bot, they're the right vendor and we're not even in the room.

**We differentiate on:** on-device + zero-integration + self-serve per-seat (vs. cloud enterprise platforms + sales cycles), and consumer-grade product craft. We should *not* try to win the call-center/voice-bot enterprise market — that's their turf and a different company.

**Watch item:** a **Jio/Reliance-backed consumer** Indian voice product (via Reverie) would be a Google-scale distribution threat in India. Low signal today, but the one to watch in this group.
